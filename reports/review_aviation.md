# Aviation domain review: GROUNDED NO MORE

Reviewer stance: a senior airline materials planning manager (hub carrier and MRO background: rotable pools, AOG desk, repair and warranty, line-station stocking) sitting on a hiring panel. Scope: README, METHODOLOGY, LIMITATIONS, STUDY_GUIDE, MODEL_CARD, `config/*.yaml`, `docs/DECISIONS.md`, `docs/agent_notes/*.md`, the exported `docs/data/*.json`, the history parquet tables, and the live UI text of all 8 workspaces plus Data & Method (rendered locally with Playwright from `docs/`).

Numbers quoted below come from the repo's own outputs: holdout P0 2,871 material AOG h/yr, I125 223 h/yr, capital AED 36.8M vs 41.9M; history (P0, weeks 0–103) 180 AOG incidents, 5,507 h.

Severity key: **critical** = a panel member would stop the interview on it or it undermines the headline result; **major** = a credible materials manager would object and it needs fixing before you show the project; **minor** = wording, consistency or polish.

## What's genuinely strong

- **Integrity scaffolding is better than most professional studies I've reviewed.** Planning code is barred from ground truth by a test. One policy-agnostic engine runs every policy on common random numbers. Calibration was locked before any P1/P2 existed. Freeze hashes precede a single holdout run, and the holdout incident was disclosed (D010). The frozen MASE champion coming 9th of 9 is reported rather than hidden (D011).
- **Correct core vocabulary in the glossary.** MTBUR vs MTBF and why NFF makes MTBUR the spares metric; serviceable vs U/S; core; condemnation/BER; exchange vs pool; the AOG desk; outstation vs hub; line vs base. D0/D3/D10 are consistently labelled "hypothetical, not MEL" across docs and UI.
- **Rotable mechanics are right in principle.** Serialised states, a core return leg from the outstations, vendor transit, condemnation triggering a replacement PO, Palm's theorem on total pipeline time, EBO convexity driving the greedy, and the POL-6 catch (Palm's S is total spares, not shelf stock), found by a Palm-vs-sim check.
- **Hub-and-spoke realism in places.** Deferred D3/D10 removals at outstations are mostly rectified on the next hub return (SIM-11). D10 needs ship routine when the window allows. Backorders are served most urgent first, and D0 can break kit reservations (SIM-6). Lateral donors keep a protection level (AOG-3).
- **The AOG Response Lab is the best piece.** It evaluates options at request time with only what the desk knew, at quoted dates (perturbation-tested). It never auto-ranks robbery and sends it to "engineering/quality approval, human decision" with owner/lessor and records checks.
