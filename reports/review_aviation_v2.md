# Aviation domain review: v2 world

**Reviewer stance:** a senior airline materials planning manager on a hiring panel. Background: rotable pools, AOG desk, repair and warranty, line-station stocking, AMOS/SAP-style planning at a Gulf hub carrier and an MRO.
**Scope:** v2 only, judged on its own merits. **Checks against the data:** I queried `data/processed/grounded.duckdb` (the P0 history, weeks 0–103). Numbers marked "(DB)" come from my own queries.

---

## Verdict in one paragraph

This is far better than the usual portfolio piece. The applicability and QPA work is good. The freeze and holdout discipline is real. The candidate discloses more weaknesses than most practitioners would. But the headline result (P0 1,690 h → P2 210 h, −88%, "for +AED 0.6M") would not survive ten minutes with an AOG desk manager, for three reasons:

1. **Robbery is half-booked.** Robbed donor aircraft are kept on the ground for days, and none of those hours count as AOG.
2. **The legacy baseline fails in ways no real line-station list would.** Six of its eight outstation units are brakes that are never changed outside AUH, and it never re-provisions in two years despite seeing 2.5× the quoted removals on its top driver.
3. **"Positioning the existing float" is not what P2 does.** Going from P0 to P2 means buying 68 rotables (AED 8.4M) and shedding 20 high-value ones.

The method is sound. The size of the effect is mostly an artefact of how the baseline and the recovery routes were built.

## Plausibility check of the headline numbers

- **P0 1,690 h vs P2 210 h a year (40 aircraft, 120 PNs).** Nearly all P0 downtime is short D0 events at outstations: median 14 h, p90 32 h, max 83 h (DB). The pattern is "no-go part not held at the station → next flight from AUH (9–16 h) plus fit". Stock those parts at the stations and most of it disappears. The direction is right and the size is inflated by AV2-01, -02, -04, -06 and -09. With a credible P0 (a line kit plus annual re-provisioning) I would expect the gap to be a fraction of 1,480 h. The project's own in-memory V3 variant already took about 230 h off P0 in the history, despite holding 150 kit units.
- **I125 at +AED 7.6M for −134 h/yr.** The rule is applied honestly, but the capital is understated (AV2-13). The additions include dead outstation brakes (AV2-05) and more D0 actuators justified by PS1-type reliability (AV2-06). Treat it as illustrative.
- **Top AOG drivers.** All 11 urgent PNs are D0, five of them landing-gear items. A real Gulf carrier's list would also feature deferral-expiry items (IDG/VFSG, bleed, packs, fuel pumps, ADIRU). Their absence here is a consequence of AV2-07 and AV2-08, not a finding.
- **Cost assumptions.** The AOG rates (AED 15k/30k per hour plus 40k/100k per event), 20% carrying, 8–25% repair cost and AED 2–15k emergency logistics are defensible scenario values and are sensitivity-tested. Unit costs are the weak input (AV2-13).

## Are the headline claims honest and well framed?

Mostly yes in tone and no in emphasis. The project discloses the route dependence, the straw-man line-station rule, the penalty scenario, the post-plan check and the forecasting champions' poor holdout showing. That is unusually honest. What still misleads:

- "Positioning the existing float" and "+AED 0.6M" (AV2-03).
- The uncounted donor downtime (AV2-01).
- The stale "holdout seeds 1000–1019" label on the recommendation screen (AV2-10).
- "Realistic starting point" for a band chosen after the fact (AV2-11).

Fix those four and the framing would be panel-proof even before any re-run.

## What's genuinely strong

- **Applicability and QPA are now right.** Fleet-exclusive parts, sensible QPAs (A320: 4 elevator and aileron servos, 10 spoiler servos, 8 ADMs, 3 ADIRUs; 787: 4 VFSGs, 4 CACs, 4 EMPs, 8 brakes and EBACs), no ATA 36 on the 787 archetype with electrical analogues instead, and only genuinely common hardware shared.
- **Rectification-window discipline.** No D0 consumables, NONE for fluids, D0 only where relief is implausible, explicit "not an approved MEL" wording, and the elapsed-hours vs calendar-days nuance disclosed.
- **Brakes handled as wear-planned demand with notice**, sourced at notice when the hub is short. Cost-per-landing contracts are acknowledged.
- **NFF and condemnation** are modelled with sensible shares (10–40% NFF for avionics and computers, 0 for brakes and wheels; 1–8% BER), with short NFF shop visits that never condemn. A real planner would recognise these.
- **No AOG outright buy and no pool growth.** Borrowed units are never owned or capital and are returned. Robbery is flagged for approval in the lab. This fixes the classic "AOG desk magically grows the float" error.
- **Palm/EBO maths done properly.** Fill is P(X ≤ S−1), not the ready rate. The P0 protection fix (V2-SIM-10) shows the author understands the off-by-one. Total spares include the repair loop, so capital is comparable across policies.
- **Hub vs outstation logic.** The P2 rule (an outstation unit only while the avoided miss cost per AED beats the carrying rate, with the miss priced through the actual recovery routes by station and fleet) is exactly how a network planner should think. The P1 vs P2 comparison is a good teaching point despite AV2-24.
- **Evaluation discipline.** P0-only calibration locked before any policy run, a frozen and hashed holdout on fresh seeds, paired CRN seeds, every result re-scored in one engine, routes-off and penalty sensitivities, and the post-plan check labelled as such. That is better than most airline business cases I have approved.
- **The AOG Response Lab** uses only information available at the time, never ranks robbery, and lists the human checks (Form 1/8130-3, effectivity/SB status, customs, lessor approval). The counterfactual's honest finding (the desk already takes the best route; the lever is positioning) is the right lesson.
- **The Planner Action Centre** raises realistic exception types (donor not reinstated, lead-time drift with a supplier-change rule, overdue RO, outstation depletion, condemned awaiting replacement), each with an owner and evidence. A materials team would recognise this as a weekly worklist.
- **The study guide's glossary and interview answers are accurate and appropriately humble.** Exchange vs loan is defined correctly, MTBUR vs MTBF and NFF are explained, and the AMOS/SAP vocabulary is mapped with a clear "never claim using them" warning.
