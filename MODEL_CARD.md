# Model card

Covers the two model families in GROUNDED NO MORE: the **demand forecasting models** and the **inventory policy models** (rotable float, reorder points, network positioning and the Readiness Frontier optimiser). Method details are in [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md); known gaps are in [LIMITATIONS.md](LIMITATIONS.md).

Result values are computed from `outputs/` and `docs/data/` by `scripts/render_docs.py` (template: `docs/templates/MODEL_CARD.md.tmpl`). Headline results are from the v2 holdout (seeds 2000–2019, central world); selection-seed results (100–119) are labelled. A v1 world was archived after a domain review; see [archive/v1](archive/v1/README.md).

## 1. Forecasting models

### Intended use

Produce weekly demand forecasts and lead-time demand distributions for spare parts with sparse, intermittent demand, so that reorder points and floats can be set. Compare methods fairly by demand class. Show how an exposure-based reliability model compares with time-series methods for removal-driven parts.

### Models

| Model | Inputs | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Zero (benchmark only), naive, moving average | Weekly demand history | Point forecast |
| Croston, SBA, TSB, ADIDA, IMAPA | Weekly demand history | Point forecast (demand rate) |
| Bootstrap (Willemain-style) | Weekly demand history | Lead-time demand distribution |
| Reliability-exposure (gamma–Poisson, prior = 2 pseudo-removals at the quoted MTBUR) | Removal history, flight exposure (FH/FC), installed quantity, vendor-quoted MTBUR | Removal rate and negative binomial lead-time demand |

### Training and evaluation data

- Train on weeks 0–77, select on six rolling origins 78–98 (4-week horizon), evaluate once after the freeze on 12 origins (weeks 104–148) of the history continuation.
- History generated by the event engine under the legacy OEM-quote policy P0 on `history_seed = 7`, in the calibrated, locked v2 world.
- Demand is unconstrained (removals are recorded whether or not stock existed).

### Caveats

- Performance is measured on data whose generating process the author designed. A method that matches the generator's assumptions (the reliability model on exposure-driven removals) has a structural advantage that may not carry over to real data. NFF removals (V2-SIM-4) are generated but still follow exposure.
- The vendor-quoted MTBUR error is ASSUMED and was set by the P0 calibration (V2-SIM-CAL). Real quoted-versus-achieved gaps vary widely by component and operator.
- In rolling-origin scoring the reliability model is given the realised flight exposure of the forecast weeks (a known flying programme). The planning forecasts that feed the policies use a trailing 52-week exposure instead.
- The training window is two years; parts with very few removals lean on the quoted MTBUR.
- Champions were best in only 3 of 9 cells on the holdout; the intermittent-rotable champion (SBA) ranked 6th of 9. Rotable policies use the reliability rate instead.

## 2. Inventory policy models

### Intended use

Recommend rotable floats, consumable and expendable reorder points, and hub-versus-outstation positioning that trade spares capital against expected material-related downtime. Show the trade-off as a frontier, explain each unit of stock, and test recommendations in simulation before presenting them.

### Models

| Component | Method |
|---|---|
| Baseline P0 | Legacy OEM-quote provisioning: Poisson protection 95% for D0 and 90% for D3/D10 on quoted data, held at AUH, a line-station unit only for D0 parts with at least 4 quoted removals a year at that station |
| Rotable float | Palm's theorem: pipeline ~ Poisson(λ · E[T]); EBO and fill P(X ≤ S − 1) by stock level; λ from the reliability-exposure posterior |
| Reorder points | Lead-time demand quantile at the rectification-window target (D0 0.97, D3 0.92, D10 0.85; ASSUMED); S = s + EOQ |
| Positioning (P2) | Pooled hub float on network demand; an outstation unit is added while its avoided miss cost (v2 AOG routes, hourly rate, cost per event) per AED of unit cost is at least the 20% carrying rate. METRIC-inspired, not full METRIC |
| Readiness Frontier | Greedy marginal allocation within stated guardrails (G1 99.5% fill cap per stocking point, G2 one unit at low-rate outstations). A heuristic |
| Referee | Policy-agnostic event engine with the v2 AOG routes, paired seeds, frozen parameters |

### Metrics (holdout, 20 paired seeds, 52 weeks, central world)

| Metric | P0 | P2 | P2 − P0 (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediate fill rate | 98.9% | 97.7% | −1.25 pts [−1.42 to −1.09] |
| Material AOG hours / yr | 1,690 h | 210 h | −1,480 h [−1,629 to −1,332] |
| Spares capital | AED 48.4M | AED 49.0M | +AED 0.61M [0.56–0.67] |
| Total annual cost | AED 69.7M | AED 35.8M | −AED 33.9M [−37.9 to −29.9] |
| Outstation rotable units | 8 | 44 | |

- **Core finding:** **Network positioning (P2) cuts material AOG by 1,480 h a year [1,332–1,629] vs a legacy OEM-quote policy holding only 8 outstation rotables, for about +AED 0.6M of spares capital.** P2 holds 44 rotables at the six outstations instead of 8 and pools the rest at the hub. Material AOG falls from 1,690 h to 210 h a year (88% lower) on the holdout (seeds 2000–2019, paired 95% CI). It holds in all three calibrated worlds and across the ×0.5–×2 range of the hourly AOG penalty.
- **Across worlds** (P1/P2 re-planned per world): low stress −1,263 h [−1,443 to −1,084] at −AED 1.0M capital; central −1,480 h [−1,629 to −1,332] at +AED 0.6M capital; high stress −1,619 h [−1,800 to −1,438] at +AED 3.0M capital.
- **Frontier option I125** (holdout, paired vs P2): AED 7.6M more capital, −134 h [−181 to −87] material AOG a year, total annual cost AED 3.3M [1.8–4.8] lower.
- Analytic model vs simulation: the analytic model predicts 2,507 h a year for P0 against 1,690 h simulated on the holdout, 130 h against 210 h for P2 and 39 h against 76 h for I125. It overstates P0 and understates the network plans, so it is a ranking heuristic, not a forecast of AOG hours; it ranks well (r = 0.97 across the 25 frontier points on the holdout, r = 0.97 on selection seeds).
- Greedy vs exact optimum: on a small separable, convex toy case the greedy allocation equals the brute-force optimum at every greedy breakpoint (unit test `tests/test_optim_greedy.py::test_greedy_equals_brute_force_at_breakpoints`, passing); no exact comparison exists for the full, non-separable network problem.
- Palm vs simulation for rotable floats (P1, selection seeds): at AUH mean Palm fill 0.951 vs simulated immediate fill 0.901 over 40 rotables (mean absolute gap 0.063): the simulated hub is below Palm because the next serviceable unit first reinstates robbed donors and hands back loans; at outstations 0.985 vs 0.997, because Palm with a local repair loop ignores resupply from AUH.

### Caveats

- **The AOG routes carry every policy.** Robbery, local borrow, exchange/loan and local buy are ASSUMED markets: with the routes switched off (selection seeds, a structural stress) material AOG rises from 1,727 h to 42,070 h a year under P0, from 235 h to 18,320 h under P2 and from 92 h to 11,080 h under I125.
- The AOG cost per hour and per event is a planning scenario. P2's lead over P0 holds across ×0.5–×2 the hourly rate (×0.5 −AED 19.0M [−21.1 to −16.8]; ×1 −AED 33.9M [−37.9 to −29.9]; ×2 −AED 63.8M [−71.5 to −56.2]), but the frontier pick moves: on the selection seeds I125's net annual benefit over P2 is +AED 1.7M [0.7–2.7] at hourly penalty ×0.5 and +AED 8.0M [4.6–11.5] at ×2, against +AED 3.8M at ×1, and the rule's pick moves to I110 at ×0.5 and B150 at ×2 the hourly rate.
- I125's cost lead over P2 is not significant if repair is 25% faster (selection seeds: +AED 0.36M [−0.75 to 1.46] a year, though it still saves 45 h [14–76] of material AOG).
- P1 and P2 fill fewer requests from the shelf than P0 (−1.25 pts for P2), because their consumable and expendable reorder points are lean; this was reported, not tuned away (V2-POL-8).
- Selection-to-holdout: for P2 the selection-seed value of 6 of 6 headline KPIs lies inside the holdout 95% CI (material AOG 235 h on selection, 210 h [146–274] on holdout); I125 scored 92 h on selection and 76 h [50–102] on holdout (inside the CI).
- Rectification windows D3/D10 are simplified analogues of MEL repair-interval categories B and C, not operator MEL decisions.
- Results hold under the simulated world's assumptions. They show the method and the size of effect it can produce, not what a real airline would gain.

## Out-of-scope use

- **Not an approved maintenance, airworthiness or dispatch tool.** Not for deciding aircraft serviceability, deferrals, MEL/CDL relief or part eligibility.
- Not for setting stock levels at a real operator without re-estimating every parameter from that operator's own data and validating against its history.
- Not a reliability programme. Simulated removal rates are not measured reliability and must not be quoted as such.
- Not a procurement, contract, exchange-pricing or pool-pricing tool.
- Not for comparing real airlines, suppliers or MRO providers.

---

## Ethical and integrity notes

- All airline data is synthetic. The airline, its tails and its part numbers are fictional.
- No real airline, OEM or vendor data is used, and no real airline is named in product text.
- Every number shown carries a provenance tag (REAL, DERIVED, ASSUMED, SIMULATED).
- P0 alone was calibrated and locked before any data-driven policy existed; the holdout was frozen and hashed before it was run, on fresh seeds, so results were not tuned against it.
