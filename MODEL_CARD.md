# Model card

Covers the two model families in GROUNDED NO MORE: the **demand forecasting models** and the **inventory policy models** (rotable float, reorder points, network positioning and the Readiness Frontier optimiser). Method details are in [METHODOLOGY.md](METHODOLOGY.md); known gaps are in [LIMITATIONS.md](LIMITATIONS.md).

Result values are computed from `outputs/` and `docs/data/` by `scripts/render_docs.py` (template: `docs/templates/MODEL_CARD.md.tmpl`). Headline results are from the holdout (seeds 1000–1019, central variant).

## 1. Forecasting models

### Intended use

Produce weekly demand forecasts and lead-time demand distributions for spare parts with sparse, intermittent demand, so that reorder points and floats can be set. Compare methods fairly by demand class. Show how an exposure-based reliability model compares with time-series methods for removal-driven parts.

### Models

| Model | Inputs | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Zero (benchmark only), naive, moving average | Weekly demand history | Point forecast |
| Croston, SBA, TSB, ADIDA, IMAPA | Weekly demand history | Point forecast (demand rate) |
| Bootstrap (Willemain-style) | Weekly demand history | Lead-time demand distribution |
| Reliability-exposure (gamma–Poisson, prior = 2 pseudo-removals at the quoted MTBUR) | Removal history, flight exposure (FH/FC), installed quantity, vendor-quoted MTBUR | Removal rate and negative binomial lead-time demand |

### Training and evaluation data

- Train on weeks 0–77, select on weeks 78–101 (six rolling origins 78–98, 4-week horizon), evaluate once after the freeze on 12 origins (weeks 104–148) of the history continuation to week 155.
- History generated by the event engine under legacy policy P0 on `history_seed = 7`.
- Demand is unconstrained (removals are recorded whether or not stock existed).

### Caveats

- Performance is measured on data whose generating process the author designed. A method that matches the generator's assumptions (for example, the reliability model on FH-driven removals) has a structural advantage that may not carry over to real data.
- The vendor-quoted MTBUR error (lognormal, 25% optimistic on average) is ASSUMED. Real quoted-versus-achieved gaps vary widely by component and operator.
- In the rolling-origin scoring (selection and holdout) the reliability model is given the realised flight exposure of the forecast weeks, a known flying programme; no other method gets future information. The fleet is fixed and weekly flying varies by about 6%, so the advantage is likely small, but it slightly favours the reliability model in the comparison above. The planning forecasts that feed the policies use a trailing 52-week exposure instead.
- The training window is two years. Parts with very few removals are classed `insufficient_data` and lean on the quoted MTBUR.
- No demand censoring is modelled. Real consumable issue history is often censored by stockouts.

## 2. Inventory policy models

### Intended use

Recommend rotable floats, consumable and expendable reorder points, and hub-versus-outstation positioning that trade spares capital against expected AOG hours. Show the trade-off as a frontier, explain each unit of stock, and test recommendations in simulation before presenting them.

### Models

| Component | Method |
|---|---|
| Rotable float | Palm's theorem: pipeline ~ Poisson(λ · E[T]), always Poisson for rotables; EBO and fill probability by stock level. E[T] from history repair orders (transport + quoted TAT + observed lateness, with condemnation via the replacement lead time). |
| Reorder points | Lead-time demand quantile (Poisson, negative binomial when overdispersed, or bootstrap) at the deadline-class service target (D0 0.97, D3 0.92, D10 0.85; ASSUMED); S = s + EOQ |
| Positioning (P2) | Pooled hub float on network demand; outstation units added while their AOG-cost saving per AED of unit cost is at least the 20% carrying rate, with hub wait EBO/λ (Little's law). METRIC-inspired, not full METRIC |
| Readiness Frontier | Greedy marginal allocation: most AOG-risk reduction per AED first. A heuristic. |
| Referee | Policy-agnostic event engine, paired seeds, frozen parameters |

### Metrics (holdout, 20 paired seeds, 52 weeks)

| Metric | P0 | P2 | P2 − P0 (95% CI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Immediate fill rate | 95.4% | 98.0% | +2.54 pp [2.27–2.81] |
| AOG hours / yr | 2,871 h | 533 h | −2,338 h [−2,709 to −1,967] |
| Spares capital (AED) | AED 36.8M | AED 37.0M | +AED 0.22M [−0.17 to 0.62] |
| Recommended I125: AOG hours / yr, capital (paired vs P2) | — | 223 h [174–272], AED 41.9M | −310 h [−408 to −212] vs P2 |

- Analytic model vs simulated AOG hours (calibration of the optimiser's proxy): for P2 the model predicts 321 h a year against 533 h simulated on the holdout; for I125 26 h against 223 h; for P0 3,017 h against 2,871 h. The proxy is a ranking heuristic, not a forecast of AOG hours, and it ranks well: correlation r = 0.95 across the 23 frontier points (r = 0.96 on selection seeds).
- Greedy vs exact optimum on a small enumerable case: on a small separable, convex toy case the greedy allocation equals the brute-force optimum at every greedy breakpoint (unit test `tests/test_optim_greedy.py::test_greedy_equals_brute_force_at_breakpoints`, passing); no exact comparison exists for the full, non-separable network problem.
- Palm vs simulation for rotable floats (P1, selection seeds): at AUH mean Palm availability 0.928 vs simulated immediate fill 0.929 over 40 rotables (mean absolute gap 0.046); at outstations 0.982 vs 0.999, because Palm with a local repair loop ignores the 4-day resupply from AUH.

### Caveats

- The objective is an analytic proxy (deadline-weighted EBO). Interval merging, finite shop capacity and hub-station coupling mean the proxy and the simulation can disagree; the calibration row shows by how much.
- The AOG penalty per hour is a planning scenario, not a measured cost. The recommended point did not move at ×0.5 or ×2 on the selection seeds, but its net value did: on the selection seeds the rule still picks I125 at both settings; its net annual benefit over P2 is AED 2.4M [1.7–3.1] at penalty ×0.5 and AED 12.1M [9.2–14.9] at ×2, against AED 5.6M at ×1.
- The recommendation is insurance against a harsher reliability world: I125 is not worth its extra capital over P2: on the selection seeds it saved 3.5 AOG h/yr [−3.9 to 10.8] versus P2 at a net annual cost of AED 0.95M [0.73–1.17], and the rule picks P2 there (the frontier holdout ran the central variant only).
- Every policy depends on the AOG-desk emergency purchase: with it switched off (selection seeds) material AOG rises from 428 h to 14,400 h a year under P2, from 118 h to 11,230 h under I125, and from 2,938 h to 268,500 h under P0.
- Selection-to-holdout drift: the recommended point scored 118 h on selection seeds and 223 h on the holdout (+89%); P2 went from 428 h to 533 h (+24%). The selection-seed figure for the chosen plan was optimistic, but its paired improvement over P2 replicated (−310 h [−383 to −238] on selection, −310 h [−408 to −212] on holdout).
- Deadline classes are hypothetical, not MEL categories.
- Results hold under the simulated world's assumptions. They show the method and the size of effect it can produce, not what a real airline would gain.

## Out-of-scope use

- **Not an approved maintenance, airworthiness or dispatch tool.** Not for deciding aircraft serviceability, deferrals, MEL/CDL relief or part eligibility.
- Not for setting stock levels at a real operator without re-estimating every parameter from that operator's own data and validating against its history.
- Not a reliability programme. Simulated removal rates are not measured reliability and must not be quoted as such.
- Not a procurement, contract or pool-pricing tool.
- Not for comparing real airlines, suppliers or MRO providers.

## Ethical and integrity notes

- All airline data is synthetic. The airline, its tails and its part numbers are fictional.
- No real airline, OEM or vendor data is used, and no real airline is named in product text.
- Every number shown carries a provenance tag (REAL, DERIVED, ASSUMED, SIMULATED).
- The holdout is frozen and hashed before it is run, so results were not tuned against it.
