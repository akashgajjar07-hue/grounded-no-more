# Decision log

Every non-obvious choice, with the reasoning behind it. Newest last.

| # | Date | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| D001 | 2026-11-26 | Python package `gnm` uses a `src/` layout. The data contract lives at `src/gnm/schemas.py` rather than `src/schemas.py`. | Importable, testable package. The brief's `src/schemas.py` intent is kept: one schema module is the source of truth. |
| D002 | 2026-11-26 | Policies are **parameter tables** (`PolicySpec`) run by **one** policy-agnostic event engine. | Paired comparisons need identical mechanics. Policy differences then come only from parameters and network rules, never from different code paths. |
| D003 | 2026-11-26 | Exogenous demand (flights, removals, kits, line usage) and common random numbers (repair durations, condemnation flags, lead times) are pre-sampled into a `Scenario`. | Common random numbers across policies: P0/P1/P2 see exactly the same world for each seed. |
| D004 | 2026-11-26 | No exposure feedback: a grounded aircraft's schedule still accrues exposure (protection flying is assumed), and lost flying is costed through the AOG penalty. | Keeps demand exogenous for paired evaluation. Disclosed in LIMITATIONS. |
| D005 | 2026-11-26 | Part master publishes a *vendor-quoted* MTBUR (true × lognormal noise, 25% optimistic on average). True MTBUR lives only in `data/ground_truth/`. | Realistic (quoted reliability ≠ in-service reality), and it gives the reliability-exposure model something real to beat. |
| D006 | 2026-11-26 | Material requests record **unconstrained** demand (removals happen whether or not stock exists). | Forecasting sees true demand rather than censored sales, which is how removal-driven demand works in aviation. |
| D007 | 2026-11-26 | The history (weeks 0–103) is generated by running the engine under P0 Simple Rulebook rules on `history_seed`. | Gives realistic ERP-style tables (POs, ROs, transfers, AOG) that the planner screens can show. |
| D008 | 2026-11-26 | Holdout = 52-week horizon on seeds 1000–1019 (policies) plus the history seed's continuation (forecasting). Both are run once, after the freeze hash. | The brief's holdout discipline. |
| D009 | 2026-11-26 | Requirement deadline classes D0/D3/D10 are labelled hypothetical and never called MEL categories. | Integrity rule. Real MEL relief is operator-specific. |
| DOC-1 | 2026-11-26 | Result numbers in prose docs are written as `{{RESULT:<dotted.key>}}` result keys. Keys name the output they come from (for example `policies.P2.aog_hours`, `frontier.recommended.capital_aed`). The full list is in internal development notes (kept privately). | No fabricated numbers. One grep (`grep -rn "{{RESULT:" *.md`) shows what is still unfilled before release. |
| DOC-2 | 2026-11-26 | `data/data_dictionary.md` and `docs/assumption_register.md` are generated by `scripts/render_data_dictionary.py` from `schemas.py` and `assumptions.yaml`, never edited by hand. | The docs cannot drift from the contract. |
| DOC-3 | 2026-11-26 | Docs use two availability terms: P(X ≤ S) = "ready rate" (no backorder at a random moment) and P(X < S) = fill rate (an arriving Poisson demand finds stock). | The brief writes "availability P(X<S)"; both appear in the literature, so both are named explicitly to avoid an off-by-one. |
| FC-1 | 2026-11-26 | Syntetos–Boylan classes on weekly buckets of the training window (weeks 0–77) only. ADI = mean gap between successive non-zero weeks (the gap before the first demand is left-censored and not counted); CV² uses population std of non-zero sizes; < 3 non-zero weeks → `insufficient_data`. ABC by annual usage value (training mean × 52 × unit cost), A while the cumulative share above the part is < 80 %, B < 95 %, else C. | The thresholds and buckets are fixed before any selection-window data is seen, so the classes cannot leak. With fewer than 2 observed intervals, ADI and CV² are noise. |
| FC-2 | 2026-11-26 | Smoothing parameters (Croston/SBA/TSB grid {0.05…0.3}), MA window and bootstrap transitions are fitted per origin on data before the origin only. MASE scale = in-sample lag-1 naive MAE on the same data; a zero scale gives NaN and the flag `undefined`, never inf. | Hyndman & Koehler (2006) scaling, made explicit; a leak test perturbs the future and asserts identical forecasts and parameters. |
| FC-3 | 2026-11-26 | Champion per demand_class × part_class = lowest mean MASE over parts (part = mean over the 6 selection origins 78…98, h = 4), ties within 0.001 broken by smallest abs(mean bias), then simpler method. Cells with < 5 parts with defined MASE fall back to the pooled demand_class, then to the global pool. Evaluation weeks are never read. | Selection discipline; small cells would otherwise pick a champion on 1–2 parts. |
| FC-4 | 2026-11-26 | The `zero` forecast is scored as a benchmark but is not eligible as champion. | On intermittent series, MAE-type metrics (MASE included) reward an all-zero forecast, which implies holding no stock. On Car Parts, `zero` has the lowest mean MASE (see internal development notes (kept privately)), so the exclusion is a choice made openly, not hidden. |
| FC-5 | 2026-11-26 | Reliability-exposure prior: gamma(k, k·MTBUR_quoted), default k = 2 pseudo-removals. Future exposure is the planned flying programme (input). | The vendor quote is optimistic (D005), so a weak prior lets about 2 years of removals dominate while stabilising parts with very few removals. |
| FC-6 | 2026-11-26 | Willemain bootstrap jitter uses nearest-integer rounding (J = round(X + Z√X), J < 1 → X) instead of the original 1 + INT(·). | The original form adds about +0.5 units per demand on average. |
| SIM-1 | 2026-11-26 | Flight schedule and A-check kit plan are world-level (depend on `world_seed` only); scenario seeds vary only demand and common random numbers. Demand streams are one `SeedSequence([salt, seed, departure_week])` per week, ids are encoded by need-week (`removal_id = week·10⁴ + rank`, `request_id = week·10⁵ + rank`). | A planned schedule is not random across seeds. Per-week streams make a weeks 104–155 scenario identical to the same slice of a longer range without generating anything earlier (tested). |
| SIM-2 | 2026-11-26 | Flight hours come from great-circle distance × 1.05 / airborne speed (830 km/h NB, 870 km/h WB); turnaround is set so each tail hits its planned FH/day. FC/day follows geography: NB ≈ 4.2 (config 4.2), WB ≈ 2.4 (config 1.6), because the AUH–LHR/FRA/BOM/SIN mix averages ~5.6 FH per leg, not 8.75. `aircraft_master.fc_per_day_mean` is the geometry-consistent value. | Can't match both the WB FH and FC means with the configured routes. FH (the main reliability driver) is matched and the FC gap is disclosed. |
| SIM-3 | 2026-11-26 | NB overdispersion is a fleet-wide gamma frailty per (PN, week): weekly fleet counts are NB with var = μ + μ²/k. | Per-leg NB draws would average the overdispersion away, and forecasting works on weekly buckets. |
| SIM-4 | 2026-11-26 | Shop `capacity_slots` are bench/test-stand positions. A unit holds a slot for `engine.shop_bench_time_share` (0.05) of its work time; the rest of the work time runs outside the slot. | Taken literally, 14 slots at 15–90-day TAT can process fewer than 200 repairs a year against about 1,100 rotable removals. 0.05 was chosen on the first P0 history (about 0.8 bench utilisation at AUH_INHOUSE). On the calibrated history it gives about 0.68 at AUH and 0.30 at EU_VENDOR. |
| SIM-5 | 2026-11-26 | Expedite POs: lead = min(0.5 × drawn lead, cap by class {C: 2, E: 3, R: 5} days) at a 15% premium. They are raised at once for D0 backorders and just in time for D3/D10 (deadline − expedite time − 24 h). Each is netted against open expedite POs and repair returns already in work that are due back within the expedite time. | Without caps and netting, P0 histories showed ~50k AOG h/yr and runaway emergency buying. These are AOG-desk distributor and surplus-market buys, documented as ASSUMED. |
| SIM-6 | 2026-11-26 | Backorders are served most urgent first (earliest need_h + deadline). Kit reservations protect stock except against needs already AOG (D0 at need time, D3/D10 after their deadline), which may rob a kit. A D10 need shipped from stock goes by routine transport, because the deadline exceeds the routine mean + 2 sd; D0/D3 needs go emergency. | This is how a materials controller allocates. Strict FIFO with hard reservations had D0 requests waiting days behind kit claims that were three weeks out. |
| SIM-7 | 2026-11-26 | Outstation stock-transfer orders (STO) on AUH: the outstation IP includes pending STOs. When an AUH unit is shipped earmarked to an outstation backorder, an equal pending STO is cancelled. | Stops the backorder from being double-covered (STO + emergency shipment), which would leave the outstation above target. |
| SIM-8 | 2026-11-26 | P0 Simple Rulebook rule (lead decision). Rotables: network float = ceil(quoted annual removals × quoted shop TAT / 365), with no transport, queueing or safety stock, all at AUH except 1 unit (taken from the float) at an outstation when the PN is D0 and quoted annual removals there are ≥ 4. C/E: min = 6 weeks of quoted demand, max = 2 × min at every station with demand (AUH sized on network demand), no deadline differentiation. Quoted demand = quoted MTBUR (C: quoted FC per unit) × planned utilisation × 50/50 hub/route split + expected A-check kit demand (`gnm.sim.quoted`). | Mediocre, not absurd. Public and planning data only, never removal history. |

| AOG-1 | 2026-11-26 | An AOG incident is one `aog_events` row. Each driving request's AOG interval is [need + deadline slack, fill]; merged per incident, these reproduce `aog_events.hours` exactly (180 incidents, 5,507 h, tested). The route actually taken is traced from the filling `ISSUE` back through `TRANSFER_IN` / `RECEIPT_PO` / `RECEIPT_REPAIR` (by serial for rotables). | Every timeline step is backed by a table row, and the lab cannot drift from the history it explains. |
| AOG-2 | 2026-11-26 | Options are evaluated at `need_h` from information available then: stock from transactions before the request (plus same-hour transactions before its own first transaction); POs/ROs opened before t and still open at t, at their **quoted** dates; notified kits; trailing D0 demand; transfers in transit with ETA = ship + planned transit. Actual receipt, return and arrival times are never read. A perturbation test changes everything after t and asserts identical options. | No future information. Option (b) feasibility agrees with P0's own AUH-transfer decisions in every incident, which validates the reconstruction. |
| AOG-3 | 2026-11-26 | Lateral donor protection level = smallest n with P(Poisson(λL) ≤ n−1) ≥ 0.95, where λ = the donor's trailing-52-week D0 requests for the PN and L = mean routine transit (96 h). A donor ships only if on-hand − kit commitments − PL ≥ qty; the nearest qualifying donor is chosen. | A donor must keep cover for its own aircraft. The fill-rate form matches DOC-3, and the inputs are history the desk has at that moment. |
| AOG-4 | 2026-11-26 | `exposure_aed = max(0, wait − slack) × AOG penalty/h (archetype) + direct cost`. Direct cost = logistics + expedite premium + acceleration fee. Purchase price and repair cost are excluded, because they are capital/consumption or paid anyway. Ranking = exposure, then wait, then cost. Robbery is always shown as "requires engineering/quality approval — human decision" and never ranked. Pool is greyed `NOT_CONTRACTED` (P0 history has no pool contract; `config/aog_lab.yaml`). Accelerated repair is feasible only if it is strictly faster than every other feasible option. | Follows the brief's cost definition and keeps capital out of operating cost. Robbery moves the shortage to another aircraft and needs approvals a model cannot give. |
| AOG-5 | 2026-11-26 | The counterfactual compares the actual route with the rank-1 option, scored ex post where history records the outcome: transfers use the fixed transport matrix, and "await order" uses the order's actual arrival. If the order was not in by its quoted date, the desk re-plans to an expedite at that moment (`counterfactual.replan_at_quoted_due`). Acceleration, expedite and pool keep their estimate. Ex-ante figures are reported alongside. | Scoring a quote-based plan on quotes alone overstated savings (late ROs). Scoring it on the raw late arrival assumes a passive desk. Result: 3,641 h vs 5,531 h request-level AOG, AED 41.1 M net exposure saving over two years; laterals are the largest lever (973 h). |
| AOG-6 | 2026-11-26 | Planner Action Centre (week 103) exception rules are fixed in `config/aog_lab.yaml: actions` and the severity functions in `gnm.aog.actions`. | POL-2 | 2026-11-26 | Planning forecasts are fitted on history weeks 0–103 with the frozen per-cell champion (`outputs/forecasting/champions.csv`). The C/E lead-time demand is NB by moments: mean = champion rate × lead time; variance = VMR(last 52 weeks) × mean + rate² × σ_L², thinned to stations as 1 + π(VMR − 1). It is Poisson if variance/mean < 1.05, and the Willemain empirical LTD at the hub when the champion is the bootstrap. | Point methods carry no distribution, and Poisson wrapping under-covers. Including lead-time variance is standard planner practice. Every function takes an `origin` and uses only data before it (perturbation test). |
| POL-3 | 2026-11-26 | Rotable removal rates come from the reliability-exposure posterior (gamma prior = 2 pseudo-removals at the vendor-quoted MTBUR) × planned exposure × station share, for every rotable, whatever its MASE champion. | Palm needs a rate.
